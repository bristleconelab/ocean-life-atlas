About Ocean Life Atlas

Click the sea, see who lives there. An open, plain-language atlas of ocean life on a 3D globe, built on more than 200 million records of where marine species were seen or sampled.

Questions

What is Ocean Life Atlas?

A free, open-source 3D globe of life in the sea. Click any spot to see the animals and plants recorded within a square about 150 km across, with photos and everyday names, or search an animal to see where it lives, how deep, how it migrates and when it is seen.

Where does the data come from?

Species records, ranges, depths and temperatures come from OBIS, the Ocean Biodiversity Information System of UNESCO's Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission. Names come from the World Register of Marine Species (WoRMS), photos and everyday names from Wikimedia Commons and Wikidata, migration networks from MiCO (Bentley et al. 2025).

Why does part of the ocean look empty?

Because nobody has recorded anything there. Most of the open ocean has hardly been surveyed. The atlas shows where people have looked, how long ago, and how complete each area's species list probably is (Chao1 estimate).

Can I download the data?

Yes. Each square's species list downloads as CSV, each species range as GeoJSON, and the code that builds the atlas is MIT-licensed on GitHub. Each data source keeps its own licence.

Is it available in Uzbek?

Yes. The interface switches between English and Uzbek (EN / UZ), and animal names and Wikipedia summaries follow the chosen language where Wikidata has them.
